## Interpretation

The reference uses the forced-full semantic-validation path. A retains derived views and predicts closed-fragment outcomes, but checks full J and uses Core for complete normative evidence. B independently reconstructs the anchor once per whole-history claim and recomputes every Core step; producer and checker run in separate processes. These are conservative measured implementations, not lower bounds on all possible IVM or witness algorithms.

Existing output profiles must emit their canonical evidence bytes: output work is at least Ω(B). Full J scans and repeated prefix replay (retained in superseded campaign001) are implementation overhead, rather than mathematical necessities. The experimental DAG changes transport representation and preserves complete expansion; flat arrays and full Core evidence construction still impose substantial work. It is not an authenticated query-completeness protocol.

Nova setup, parameter loading/validation, folding, verification and the full Core bridge are separate recorded phases. Recursive proof verification can be history independent at fixed circuit capacity while combined Core verification remains dependent on the replayed prefix. Compression was not implemented.

Primary wall times are medians of contiguous whole samples. Implementation-phase totals are summed inside each individual sample before taking the median. Oracle comparison and archive retention are reported separately and must not be described as library execution speed. Unmeasured allocation/copy/parse totals remain unknown.

The initial debug pilot and early runs are not the final optimized comparison. Early001 controller-affinity nonconformance is explicitly annotated and excluded from binding performance conclusions. No published Core031 evidence was rewritten.

### Representation and storage

For completed baseline A cases, the DAG reduces raw envelope size, but gzip archives are about4.9–7.1 times larger than the corresponding flat-profile archives. Both measurements use the same retained claim and lossless container settings. This argues against adopting the current JSON-DAG/full-support encoding as a compressed cold transport/storage optimization. It does not measure persistent sharded structures, different object granularity or warm incremental support exchange.

Detailed source boundaries and avoidable work are in `source-costs.md`; the whole experiment is not a production database/replication durability benchmark.
